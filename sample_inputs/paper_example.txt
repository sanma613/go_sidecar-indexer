ABSTRACT
================================================================================

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has emerged as a foundational paradigm
for grounding large language model (LLM) outputs in factual, up-to-date
enterprise knowledge bases. However, naive single-stage dense retrieval
pipelines frequently suffer from precision degradation at scale, particularly
when corpora exceed 10 million documents or when queries require multi-hop
reasoning across heterogeneous document types. In this paper we propose
HybridRAG-Ent, a three-stage retrieval architecture combining BM25 sparse
retrieval, bi-encoder dense retrieval, and a cross-encoder re-ranking stage,
augmented with a lightweight query-routing module trained to select the
optimal retrieval path per query class. We evaluate HybridRAG-Ent across
four enterprise benchmark datasets spanning legal contracts, technical
documentation, financial filings, and clinical notes. Our system achieves
a mean Recall@10 of 0.91 and an Answer Faithfulness score of 0.87 on the
proposed benchmarks, representing improvements of 14.3% and 9.1% respectively
over a strong single-stage dense retrieval baseline. We further demonstrate
that the query router reduces average retrieval latency by 22% by avoiding
costly cross-encoder inference on simple factual queries. Code and datasets
are released at https://github.com/amaRAG-ent/hybridrag.

Keywords: retrieval-augmented generation, hybrid retrieval, BM25, dense
retrieval, cross-encoder, enterprise search, large language models, RAG,
knowledge management, query routing

================================================================================
1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

Enterprise knowledge management systems accumulate documents at rates that
outpace the capacity of human curators. Legal departments maintain thousands
of evolving contract templates; engineering organizations version millions of
lines of internal documentation; financial institutions archive decades of
filings and earnings transcripts. The ability to query these corpora using
natural language—and to receive accurate, grounded, citation-backed answers—
has become a strategic priority for organizations adopting AI tooling.

Large language models (LLMs) trained on general web corpora lack the
specialized, proprietary, and temporally current knowledge required to serve
these needs reliably. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (Lewis et al., 2020)
addresses this limitation by conditioning LLM generation on a dynamically
retrieved context window drawn from a curated document store. The model is
thus not expected to memorize domain facts but rather to synthesize retrieved
passages into coherent, attributed responses.

Despite widespread adoption, practitioners consistently report three failure
modes in production RAG deployments:

  (1) Recall failure: relevant documents are not retrieved, so the LLM either
      hallucinates or correctly states it cannot answer.

  (2) Precision failure: retrieved documents are topically adjacent but not
      the most relevant, diluting the context window and confusing the LLM.

  (3) Latency failure: re-ranking and cross-attention stages add inference
      overhead that violates interactive latency budgets (<500ms p95).

Existing literature addresses each failure mode in isolation. BM25 improves
recall for keyword-rich queries (Robertson & Zaragoza, 2009); bi-encoder
dense retrievers improve recall for semantic queries (Karpukhin et al., 2020);
cross-encoder re-rankers improve precision (Nogueira & Cho, 2019). However,
no prior work systematically combines all three into a unified enterprise
architecture with a learned query router that selects the appropriate path.

This paper makes the following contributions:

  (C1) We propose HybridRAG-Ent, a modular three-stage retrieval pipeline
       with a lightweight learned query router.

  (C2) We curate and release four enterprise-domain RAG benchmarks totaling
       180,000 question-answer pairs with document-level provenance labels.

  (C3) We conduct a comprehensive ablation study isolating the contribution
       of each pipeline stage.

  (C4) We profile end-to-end latency under realistic enterprise load patterns
       and propose a cost-accuracy operating point selection methodology.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews
related work. Section 3 describes the HybridRAG-Ent architecture. Section 4
details the benchmark construction methodology. Section 5 presents
experimental results. Section 6 discusses limitations and future work.
Section 7 concludes.

================================================================================
2. RELATED WORK
================================================================================

2.1 Sparse Retrieval

BM25 (Best Match 25) remains a remarkably competitive baseline for lexical
retrieval more than three decades after its introduction (Robertson &
Zaragoza, 2009). Its probabilistic relevance framework models term frequency
saturation and document length normalization without requiring any neural
computation. Pyserini (Lin et al., 2021) provides a widely used open-source
implementation indexed with Apache Lucene. In enterprise settings, BM25
excels at identifier-rich queries such as contract clause lookups, regulatory
citation retrieval, and product version number searches.

Learned sparse models such as SPLADE (Formal et al., 2021) extend BM25 by
using a masked language model to expand query and document representations
into high-dimensional sparse vectors, capturing vocabulary mismatch while
retaining interpretability. SPLADE-v2 achieves competitive results on BEIR
(Thakur et al., 2021) while remaining computationally tractable at retrieval
time due to inverted index compatibility.

2.2 Dense Retrieval

Dual-encoder models (Karpukhin et al., 2020; Izacard et al., 2022) map
queries and documents into a shared dense vector space, enabling approximate
nearest-neighbor (ANN) search via libraries such as FAISS (Johnson et al.,
2019) and ScaNN (Guo et al., 2020). Dense retrievers outperform BM25 on
paraphrased and semantically varied queries but may underperform on queries
requiring exact lexical matches or rare named entity lookup.

Domain adaptation of dense retrievers is an active research area. TART
(Asai et al., 2022) introduces task-aware dense retrieval, conditioning the
encoder on a natural language task description. GPL (Wang et al., 2022)
employs generative pseudo-labeling to adapt retrievers to new domains without
human-annotated training data. Both approaches are relevant to our enterprise
setting where annotated relevance judgments are scarce.

2.3 Re-ranking

Cross-encoder re-rankers (Nogueira & Cho, 2019) take a query-document pair
as joint input to a transformer, producing a relevance score that captures
fine-grained interaction between query and document tokens. This approach
achieves strong precision on standard benchmarks such as MS MARCO but
introduces per-candidate inference cost, making it prohibitive to apply to
the full corpus. Two-stage pipelines—retrieve then re-rank—are standard
practice (Pradeep et al., 2021).

Late interaction models such as ColBERT (Khattab & Zaharia, 2020) offer a
middle ground: token-level embeddings are precomputed offline, and query-time
interaction is computed efficiently via MaxSim operations. ColBERTv2 (Santhanam
et al., 2022) adds residual compression to reduce index storage. We include
ColBERT as a baseline in our experiments.

2.4 Hybrid Retrieval

Prior work on hybrid retrieval primarily explores linear interpolation of
BM25 and dense retrieval scores (Ma et al., 2022; Luan et al., 2021).
Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF; Cormack et al., 2009) provides a parameter-free
combination that assigns scores based on retrieval rank rather than raw score
magnitude. We build on RRF as a component within HybridRAG-Ent. Our key
novelty is the addition of a learned query router that selects or combines
retrieval paths dynamically, rather than always running all stages.

2.5 RAG Systems

The original RAG paper (Lewis et al., 2020) demonstrates that retrieval-
conditioned generation outperforms closed-book QA on knowledge-intensive tasks.
Subsequent work has explored iterative retrieval (Shao et al., 2023), self-
reflective retrieval (Asai et al., 2023), and multi-document fusion (Shi et al.,
2023). REALM (Guu et al., 2020) jointly trains the retriever and reader.
However, joint training is impractical in enterprise settings where the corpus
changes frequently and LLMs are accessed via API. HybridRAG-Ent treats the
LLM as a black-box reader and focuses on maximizing retrieval quality.

3. THE HYBRIDRAG-ENT ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

3.1 System Overview

HybridRAG-Ent consists of five main components:

  (A) Query Classifier / Router
  (B) BM25 Sparse Retrieval Index
  (C) Bi-Encoder Dense Retrieval Index
  (D) Cross-Encoder Re-Ranker
  (E) Context Assembly and LLM Reader

At inference time, an incoming natural language query q is first passed to
the Query Router (A), which predicts a retrieval path label from the set
{SPARSE, DENSE, HYBRID, RERANK}. The appropriate retrieval path is then
executed, producing a ranked candidate list. If the RERANK path is selected,
the top-k candidates from a prior stage are re-scored by the cross-encoder
(D). Finally, the top-n documents are assembled into a context window and
passed to the LLM Reader (E) with a structured prompting template that
instructs the model to cite its sources.

Figure 1 (described textually): Query arrives → Router classifies → Branch:
  SPARSE → BM25 → top-k → Context Assembly → LLM
  DENSE  → ANN search → top-k → Context Assembly → LLM
  HYBRID → BM25 + ANN → RRF merge → top-k → Context Assembly → LLM
  RERANK → HYBRID → Cross-Encoder → top-n → Context Assembly → LLM

3.2 Query Router

The query router is a fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3-small classifier (He et al.,
2021) with a four-way output head. It was trained on a synthetic dataset of
12,000 query-label pairs generated by prompting GPT-4 to produce queries
representative of each retrieval path class, followed by human verification
of 2,000 samples. Training labels were defined as follows:

  SPARSE:  Queries containing specific identifiers, version numbers, exact
           phrases, or clause references (e.g., "Section 4.2 termination
           clause" or "invoice number INV-2023-08821").

  DENSE:   Semantically paraphrased queries without exact lexical anchors
           (e.g., "what happens if either party wants to end the agreement").

  HYBRID:  Queries mixing semantic and lexical signals (e.g., "GDPR Article 17
           right to erasure in customer data contracts").

  RERANK:  Complex multi-hop or comparison queries requiring precision over
           a broad initial recall set (e.g., "how do indemnification clauses
           differ between the Acme and Globex master service agreements").

The router achieves 89.4% accuracy on a held-out test set of 1,500 queries.
Misclassifications are predominantly between HYBRID and DENSE classes, which
have overlapping characteristics. The router adds approximately 18ms of
inference latency on a single A10 GPU, which is acceptable within our budget.

3.3 BM25 Sparse Retrieval

Documents are preprocessed with a pipeline consisting of: language detection
(langdetect), sentence boundary detection (spaCy), stopword removal, and
Porter stemming. Index construction uses Pyserini with Lucene 9 on an NVMe-
backed index store. For the largest benchmark (financial filings, 8.2M docs),
index construction takes 4.1 hours on a 32-core machine and occupies 94GB on
disk. Query-time BM25 retrieval with k=100 operates at median 12ms per query.

We use BM25 parameters k1=1.2, b=0.75, which are standard defaults and were
not further tuned to avoid overfitting to the development set.

3.4 Bi-Encoder Dense Retrieval

Document embeddings are generated using E5-large-v2 (Wang et al., 2023), a
1024-dimensional text encoder trained with contrastive learning on a diverse
mixture of retrieval tasks. We selected E5-large-v2 over competing models
(BGE-large, GTE-large) based on preliminary experiments showing superior
performance on the clinical notes domain, attributed to its biomedical
pre-training mix.

Embeddings are indexed with FAISS using an IVF-PQ configuration:
  - nlist = 4096 (number of Voronoi cells)
  - nprobe = 64 (cells searched at query time)
  - PQ code size = 64 bytes (compression from 4096 to 64 bytes per vector)

This configuration achieves 0.97 recall@100 versus exact search on our
development set, with a query-time cost of median 28ms per query including
embedding inference on GPU.

For domain adaptation, we apply domain-specific contrastive fine-tuning
using GPL (Wang et al., 2022) with pseudo-labels generated by BM25 retrieval
and cross-encoder scoring. Fine-tuning runs for 5,000 steps with batch size
64 on a single A100 80GB GPU, taking approximately 3 hours per domain.

3.5 Cross-Encoder Re-Ranker

The re-ranker is a fine-tuned ms-marco-MiniLM-L-12-v2 model, selected for
its balance of re-ranking quality and inference speed. We re-rank the top-50
candidates from the HYBRID stage, applying the cross-encoder in batches of
16 pairs. On a single A10 GPU, re-ranking 50 candidates takes a median of
210ms, which is the dominant latency contributor in the RERANK path.

We experimented with larger re-rankers (MonoT5-3B, RankLLaMA) but found
diminishing returns on our benchmarks relative to the latency cost, consistent
with findings in Pradeep et al. (2021) on MS MARCO.

3.6 Context Assembly and LLM Reader

Top-n retrieved documents (n=5 by default) are assembled into a structured
context prompt. Each document chunk is prefixed with a citation key
[DOC-i], its title, source system, and date. The LLM is instructed via a
system prompt to (a) answer only from the provided context, (b) cite specific
document keys inline, and (c) explicitly state when the context is insufficient.

We use Claude 3 Opus as the default LLM reader in experiments, accessed via
API. We also report results with GPT-4-Turbo and Mistral-8x7B-Instruct for
comparison. All LLM calls use a temperature of 0.0 for deterministic output
and a maximum context window of 8,192 tokens.

4. BENCHMARK CONSTRUCTION
================================================================================

4.1 Overview

We construct four enterprise-domain benchmarks, collectively named EntRAG-
Bench. Each benchmark consists of a document corpus, a question set, and
human-annotated relevance judgments at the document-chunk level.

Table 1. EntRAG-Bench Statistics
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Domain          | Corpus Size | # Questions | Avg Q Length | Annotators
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Legal Contracts |    240,000  |    45,000   |   14.2 words |     3
Technical Docs  |  1,200,000  |    52,000   |   11.8 words |     3
Financial Filings|  8,200,000 |    48,000   |   16.7 words |     4
Clinical Notes  |    380,000  |    35,000   |   13.4 words |     4
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Total           | 10,020,000  |   180,000   |   14.0 words |   3-4
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.2 Document Collection

Legal Contracts: Sourced from a partner law firm's document management
system under a data use agreement. Documents include master service agreements,
non-disclosure agreements, lease contracts, and employment agreements. All
personally identifiable information was redacted using a combination of
rule-based NER (spaCy) and manual review. Documents span 2010–2023.

Technical Documentation: Collected from three enterprise software vendors
who shared internal Confluence and GitBook documentation corpora. Topics
include API references, deployment guides, troubleshooting runbooks, and
architecture decision records. Version history was preserved to enable
temporal retrieval evaluation.

Financial Filings: Comprises 10-K, 10-Q, and 8-K filings from S&P 500
companies for fiscal years 2015–2023, downloaded from the SEC EDGAR public
API. Documents were chunked at the section level using regex-based SEC
filing structure parsers.

Clinical Notes: Obtained under IRB protocol #2023-0441 from a regional
hospital network. Notes include discharge summaries, radiology reports, and
progress notes. De-identification was performed using Philter (Norgeot et
al., 2020) followed by expert clinical review. All notes are from 2018–2022.

4.3 Question Generation

Questions were generated using a three-stage pipeline:

  Stage 1 – Seed generation: GPT-4 was prompted with random document chunks
  to generate 3 candidate questions per chunk, targeting different cognitive
  levels (factual lookup, inference, comparison).

  Stage 2 – Filtering: Questions were filtered using a trained quality
  classifier to remove trivially answerable, unanswerable, or excessively
  long questions. The classifier was trained on 3,000 human-labeled examples.

  Stage 3 – Human review: 15% of surviving questions were reviewed by domain
  expert annotators (lawyers, engineers, financial analysts, clinicians) who
  corrected or discarded low-quality items.

4.4 Relevance Annotation

Relevance judgments were collected using a purpose-built annotation interface.
Annotators were shown a question and up to 20 candidate document chunks,
retrieved by a union of BM25 and dense retrieval results, and asked to mark
each chunk as (0) Not Relevant, (1) Partially Relevant, or (2) Highly Relevant.
Inter-annotator agreement was measured with Fleiss' kappa: mean κ = 0.71
across domains, indicating substantial agreement.

Disagreements were resolved by a senior annotator adjudicating between
conflicting labels. Final relevance labels were binarized at threshold ≥1
for evaluation.

5. EXPERIMENTS
================================================================================

5.1 Evaluation Metrics

We report the following retrieval metrics:

  Recall@k: Fraction of relevant documents appearing in the top-k results.
  We report R@10 and R@100 as primary metrics.

  NDCG@10: Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain at cutoff 10, accounting
  for rank position of relevant documents.

  MRR: Mean Reciprocal Rank, measuring how high the first relevant document
  appears in the ranked list.

For end-to-end QA quality we report:

  Answer Faithfulness (AF): Percentage of answer sentences that are
  attributable to retrieved context, scored by a fine-tuned NLI model.

  Answer Relevance (AR): Cosine similarity between the question embedding
  and answer embedding, measuring topical relevance.

  Citation Accuracy (CA): Fraction of cited document keys that correspond to
  actually relevant documents, as judged by annotators.

5.2 Baselines

We compare HybridRAG-Ent against the following systems:

  BM25-Only: Standard Pyserini BM25 with no re-ranking.
  Dense-Only: E5-large-v2 with FAISS ANN search, no re-ranking.
  ColBERT: ColBERTv2 with PLAID indexing (Santhanam et al., 2022).
  RRF-Hybrid: BM25 + Dense with RRF fusion, no router, no cross-encoder.
  HybridRAG-Ent: Our full proposed system.

HybridRAG-Ent achieves the highest scores across all retrieval metrics. The

path; queries routed to SPARSE or DENSE paths achieve 21ms and 58ms p95
respectively. The router directs 34% of queries to SPARSE, 28% to DENSE,
21% to HYBRID, and 17% to RERANK, yielding a query-weighted average latency
of 89ms p95—well within interactive budget.

Removing the dense retrieval stage causes the largest performance drop,
confirming the primacy of semantic matching for our query distribution.
Removing BM25 has a smaller but significant effect, particularly on the
Legal Contracts and Financial Filings domains where exact identifier lookup
is common. The router provides minimal precision gain when the RERANK path
is forced for all queries but substantially reduces latency; its value is
operational rather than quality-driven.

5.5 Domain-Specific Analysis

Performance varies meaningfully across domains:

Legal Contracts: R@10 = 0.924. The combination of dense retrieval for
semantic clause matching and BM25 for section reference lookup is highly
effective. The cross-encoder excels at distinguishing highly similar clause
variants across contract versions.

Technical Documentation: R@10 = 0.917. Version-aware retrieval is a
challenge; the system sometimes retrieves documentation for outdated software
versions. Metadata filtering by version number reduces this error by 31%.

Financial Filings: R@10 = 0.883. The large corpus size (8.2M documents)
increases ANN approximation error. Increasing nprobe from 64 to 128 recovers
3.1 points of R@10 at the cost of an additional 11ms query latency.

Clinical Notes: R@10 = 0.916. Domain fine-tuning of the dense encoder
provides the largest gains here (+4.8 R@10 points), reflecting the large
vocabulary gap between general pre-training data and clinical language.

6. DISCUSSION
================================================================================

6.1 Practical Deployment Considerations

Enterprise deployment of HybridRAG-Ent requires infrastructure provisioning
for three distinct services: the Lucene BM25 index server, the FAISS dense
index server, and the cross-encoder inference server. We recommend deploying
each as an independent horizontally scalable service behind a load balancer,
with the query router running as a lightweight sidecar within the API gateway.

Index update latency is a practical concern. BM25 index updates using Lucene's
near-real-time (NRT) reader can reflect new documents within seconds. Dense
index updates require re-embedding new documents and updating FAISS, which
we implement as a streaming background job processing additions in batches
of 1,000 documents every 60 seconds. The cross-encoder requires no index
maintenance. End-to-end document ingestion to retrieval availability averages
73 seconds in our production deployment.

6.2 Chunking Strategy

Document chunking has an outsized impact on retrieval quality that is
underappreciated in the literature. We evaluated four strategies:

  (1) Fixed-size: 512-token chunks with 64-token overlap.
  (2) Sentence-boundary: chunks aligned to sentence boundaries, 300-600 tokens.
  (3) Structural: chunks aligned to document structural elements (sections,
      paragraphs, table rows).
  (4) Semantic: chunks split at embedding-space discontinuities using cosine
      similarity of adjacent sentences.

Structural chunking outperforms fixed-size by 4.2 R@10 points on average,
with the largest gains in financial filings where section-level retrieval
naturally aligns with analyst query patterns. Semantic chunking performs
comparably to structural chunking but is computationally expensive during
indexing (2.3× slower). We recommend structural chunking as the default.

6.3 Failure Mode Analysis

We manually analyzed 200 cases where HybridRAG-Ent failed to retrieve a
relevant document in the top-10 results. The most frequent failure modes were:

  (1) Cross-document coreference (38%): The answer requires linking an entity
      mentioned by pronoun in one document to its full name in another.

  (2) Temporal reasoning (24%): The query asks about the most recent version
      of a policy or procedure, but older versions rank higher due to higher
      lexical overlap with historical query terms.

  (3) Implicit domain knowledge (21%): The query uses lay terms for a concept
      with a specific technical name used in the document corpus (e.g.,
      "exit clause" vs. "termination for convenience provision").

  (4) Tabular data retrieval (17%): Relevant information is encoded in tables
      and the textual representation used for embedding does not capture the
      relational structure.

These failure modes suggest directions for future work, discussed in Section 6.4.

6.4 Limitations and Future Work

HybridRAG-Ent has several notable limitations:

The query router was trained on synthetic data, which may not fully represent
the distribution of queries in a specific enterprise deployment. We recommend
collecting 500-1,000 logged queries and retraining the router with domain-
specific examples before production deployment.

The system does not support multi-hop retrieval, where answering a question
requires synthesizing information from documents that are not individually
relevant to the query. Iterative retrieval approaches (Shao et al., 2023)
are a promising direction.

Tabular data retrieval remains a persistent challenge. Future work will
explore table-aware chunking with linearized table representations and
dedicated table retrieval models.

Temporal awareness is currently handled via metadata filters but not via
learned relevance signals. Incorporating document timestamps into the re-
ranking score function is a promising direction.

================================================================================
7. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

We presented HybridRAG-Ent, a three-stage hybrid retrieval architecture for
enterprise knowledge management combining BM25 sparse retrieval, bi-encoder
dense retrieval, and cross-encoder re-ranking, orchestrated by a learned
query router. Evaluated on four enterprise-domain benchmarks totaling 180,000
question-answer pairs, HybridRAG-Ent achieves state-of-the-art retrieval
quality with a mean R@10 of 0.91 and Answer Faithfulness of 0.87, while
maintaining a query-weighted average latency of 89ms p95.

Our ablation study confirms that each pipeline stage contributes meaningfully
to overall performance, with dense retrieval being the most critical component
and the query router providing the primary latency benefit. Domain-specific
fine-tuning of the dense encoder is particularly important for specialized
corpora such as clinical notes.

We release all benchmark data, model checkpoints, and infrastructure code to
facilitate reproducible research and practical enterprise adoption.
